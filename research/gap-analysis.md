# Research-gap analysis

## Provisional integration gap
The verified first-pass studies separately examine everyday music uses, adolescent identity, musical subcultures, substance-use correlates, generational memory and Bangladeshi hybridity. This set does not establish an integrated and cross-culturally comparable estimate of relationships among locality, musical scene, generational socialization, peer environment and substance-use attitudes with Bangladesh-specific validation.

**Important:** This is a gap in the *presently sampled literature*, not evidence that no such study has ever been published. A database-complete search and citation chaining should test the novelty claim.

## Main risks
- Western-centered studies could misrepresent Bangladesh or other Asian contexts.
- Genre labels change across languages, subcultures, markets and platforms.
- Age-period-cohort confounding complicates generational explanations.
- Sensitive substance behavior can be misreported; peer association is not proof of causation.
- Urban digital users may be easier to recruit than rural and offline listeners.

## Publishable next step
First, upgrade search to a documented scoping/systematic review. Second, perform qualitative interviews and mapping of local scenes in Bangladesh. Third, test a culturally adapted survey with measured peer exposure and uncertainty estimates, with ethics clearance. No original empirical evidence currently exists for this project.
