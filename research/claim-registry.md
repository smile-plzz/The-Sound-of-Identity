# High-Impact Claim Registry

This registry records the claims most likely to be challenged because they concern causality, violence, substance use, national history, or broad social generalization. It supplements the manuscript's reference list and does not replace source reading.

| ID | Claim | Evidence basis | Risk | Final treatment |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| C01 | Genres provide cultural repertoires rather than fixed listener identities | Cross-disciplinary synthesis | Medium | Presented as the review's theoretical synthesis |
| C02 | Music preference can contribute to friendship selection | Selfhout et al. (2009); Franken et al. (2017) | Medium | Qualified as probabilistic and reciprocal |
| C03 | Peer behavior can mediate music-preference and substance-use associations | Mulder et al. (2010); Slater and Henry (2013) | High | Design limits and temporal ambiguity stated |
| C04 | Substance references in lyrics demonstrate exposure potential, not behavioral causation | Primack et al. (2008); Hall et al. (2013); Hardcastle et al. (2015) | High | Content and effects evidence kept separate |
| C05 | Dhaka metal scenes distribute participation through unequal cultural and social capital | Quader (2016) | Medium | Limited to the studied scene and period |
| C06 | Bangladesh's underground metal scene can coexist with commercial aspiration | Quader and Redden (2015) | Medium | Framed as a challenge to a universal underground/commercial binary |
| C07 | Bangladeshi hip-hop can operate as political expression | Hasan and Kundu (2022) | Medium | Does not imply all rap or listeners are activist |
| C08 | Liberation War broadcasting combined music with political messaging and morale | Hossain et al. (2019) | High | Described as a contribution, not the cause of victory |
| C09 | Music can help normalize or amplify violent political environments | McCoy (2009); Sion (2026); MacLachlan (2022); Love (2016) | High | Conditional mechanism model; no genre-wide inference |
| C10 | Music does not independently explain genocide, war, or mass protest | Comparative synthesis | High | Explicitly stated throughout Sections 9 and 13 |
| C11 | Music can support memory, recognition, and peacebuilding | Lidskog (2017); McClain Opiyo (2015); Howell (2019, 2021) | Medium | Outcomes treated as context-dependent |
| C12 | Age patterns in taste do not by themselves identify life-course effects | Bonneville-Roussy et al. (2013, 2017) | Medium | Cohort and period confounding stated |
| C13 | Gangsta rap is one formation within a heterogeneous hip-hop field | Analytical distinction and genre scholarship synthesis | Medium | Used to analyze the stereotype without endorsing it |
| C14 | Symbolic extremity in metal does not establish violent listener conduct | Analytical distinction | High | Stated as a limit on inference, not a null empirical finding |
| C15 | The proposed Bangladesh study could distinguish selection from socialization better than the current review | Methodological proposal | Medium | Clearly labeled prospective and unexecuted |
