# Literature search and screening protocol

**Version:** 0.1, 12 October 2026. **Status:** Executed exploratory search; proposed systematic search not yet executed.

## Executed discovery
Searches in a web index targeted publisher websites, PubMed and university repositories using phrases: music preferences identity personality; music adolescent identity; musical subculture neo-tribes; music geography identity; music substance use peers adolescents; Bangladesh popular music cultural hybridity fusion; Bengali rock history; generational reminiscence music. Searches prioritized publisher DOI pages, PubMed, and academic repositories. The first-pass evidence register contains nine verified, formally cited academic works and one secondary regional comparison pending metadata verification. Full texts were not systematically inspected. No claim is made about the universe of search results or study-selection completeness.

## Proposed preregisterable search
Databases: Scopus, Web of Science, PsycINFO, PubMed, RILM, JSTOR, Google Scholar (supplementary), and regional South Asian journal indexes where accessible.

Core Boolean families, adapted to each database:
1. (music OR "popular music" OR rock OR pop) AND ("cultural identity" OR "social identity" OR subcultur* OR scene*)
2. (music OR "music preference") AND (geograph* OR place OR locality OR globali* OR diaspora OR hybridity)
3. (music OR genre) AND (adolescen* OR age OR generation* OR cohort OR autobiographical memory)
4. (music OR "music preference") AND ("substance use" OR alcohol OR cannabis OR smoking) AND (peer* OR adolescen* OR confound*)
5. (Bangladesh OR Bangla OR Bengali) AND (rock OR pop OR "popular music" OR "folk fusion" OR band*)

Eligibility: scholarly books, peer-reviewed quantitative/qualitative studies and rigorous scholarly reviews directly related to one or more questions; primary focus on 1990–2026, with foundational works earlier if material; English and Bangla studies, and translated abstracts when possible. Exclude non-scholarly news from the evidence synthesis, duplicate records, unrelated therapeutic efficacy studies, and sources lacking identifiable bibliographic metadata.

## Screening and extraction
For each record capture: author/year, DOI/URL, region, genre definitions, study design, sample, age range, measured outcomes, confounders, findings, limitations, relevance to Bangladesh, accessibility and full-text verification status. Two independent reviewers and disagreement resolution are recommended for a true systematic review. Track date and database of each search, number retrieved, duplicate removal, title/abstract exclusions and full-text exclusions with reasons; construct PRISMA flow only after counts exist.

## Evidence-quality safeguards
Separate research claims from conceptual interpretations; avoid causal verbs for cross-sectional data; do not impute drug behavior from genre; distinguish Bengali music in India from Bangladeshi music; evaluate language and urban sampling bias. Never fabricate participant data, PRISMA counts, confidence intervals or bibliography metadata. Any original survey requires protocol, power/precision assessment and ethics clearance.
